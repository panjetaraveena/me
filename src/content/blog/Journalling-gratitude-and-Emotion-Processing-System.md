Journalling feels simple, but sitting down with your own thoughts can be surprisingly difficult. If you're anything like me, the natural defence mechanism is to stay busy, replay conversations, scroll endlessly, or try to “figure everything out” without actually acknowledging what you're feeling.

Avoiding an emotion can create the impression that you've moved on without actually processing anything. **By keeping yourself occupied, you postpone the discomfort—but the emotion usually returns wearing a different outfit.**

# Journalling as a processing system

**Whenever an emotion feels unusually strong, write before you react.** That's the only rule. The entry can be a page or a single sentence. You don't need perfect grammar, beautiful handwriting, or a profound conclusion.

Start with three questions:

- What happened?
- What am I feeling?
- What might this feeling be trying to tell me?

This creates a small gap between experiencing an emotion and acting on it. Sometimes that gap is all you need.

# Notice, name, navigate

A useful journal doesn't have to solve every problem. It only needs to make the problem easier to see.

Notice what happened without immediately judging it. Name the emotion as precisely as you can. Then decide how you want to navigate it: take action, have a conversation, set a boundary, ask for help, or simply let the feeling pass.

**The goal isn't to stop feeling difficult emotions. It's to stop letting unnamed emotions quietly make decisions for you.**

# Gratitude without pretending

Gratitude isn't about forcing positivity or convincing yourself that everything is fine. It's about remembering that a difficult day is rarely made of difficulty alone.

Write down one thing that helped, one thing you appreciated, or one ordinary moment you don't want to overlook. A kind message counts. A quiet cup of tea counts. Getting through the day counts.

Journalling helps you process what hurts. Gratitude helps you notice what still holds you. Together, they become a simple emotional processing system: **feel honestly, reflect clearly, appreciate deliberately, and move forward gently.**
